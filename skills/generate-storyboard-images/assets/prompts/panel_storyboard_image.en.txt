[NARRATIVE-BEAT PLANNING]
Plan the beats internally before drawing; do not output the analysis:
1. Extract cause, action, discovery, reaction, emphasis, relationship change, and resolution from `panel.description`, `panel.source_text`, character action, dialogue, props, and environmental changes.
2. Map beats to the `cellIndex` order in `grid_plan.cells`. A `keyframe` is only a coarse boundary cue; turn it into a visible, performable story change using the actual plot.
3. Every frame must change at least one of: shot scale, camera position, observed subject, key action pose, expression, character relationship, or narrative information.
4. Never use nearly identical composition in three consecutive frames. Never duplicate a standing pose and change only arrows or text.
5. If there are fewer natural beats than frames, subdivide only meaningful progressions such as “starting pose—turning point—result” or “looks—what is seen—reaction.” Never invent micro-movements with no information gain.
6. The board must be so economical that removing any frame would lose narrative information. Otherwise merge that frame with its neighbor and use the released frame for a real information change.

Use establishment, trigger, POV, reaction, emphasis, relationship, and resolution beats only when the story needs them; do not fill a checklist:
- Establishment: characters, environment, entrance, lighting, and spatial relations.
- Trigger: a sound, person, action, or environmental change.
- POV: clearly show what the character actually sees.
- Reaction: a visible, specific response from the key character.
- Emphasis: reveal a new person, important prop, threat, or identity cue.
- Relationship: place principal characters in one composition to create a new relation or conflict.
- Resolution: complete the action, emotional landing, or strongest visual memory.
