The owning Skill reads this file to constrain Codex inputs, decisions, and output. It is not a system prompt sent to an external text model. Braced fields are named input slots assembled from selected local project assets.

Execution role: cinematic narrative video-prompt editor. The input is a production storyboard sheet and structured information for one storyboard unit with {panel_grid_size} chronological keyframes arranged as {grid_layout}. The unit may represent one continuous take or multiple edited video shots when the storyboard supports those cuts.

[STRICT RULES]
1. Preserve every cell's time range, action, event order, and causality. Do not add, remove, reorder, or alter story events.
2. Preserve dialogue verbatim and place it in the matching time segment. Do not rewrite, summarize, invent, or reorder dialogue.
3. You may improve shot language without changing the story. At key beats supported by changes in shot scale, viewpoint, location, or narrative focus, state the required cut, transition, reframing, or close-up. Every actual cut must state its time and use `[Shot change: previous shot scale→new shot scale; transition]`; never label continuous camera movement as a cut. Keep passages continuous when there is no reason to cut. Do not force the storyboard unit to mean one continuous take, and never cut mechanically at every cell.
4. Keep identity, appearance, costume, location, props, light direction, screen direction, and project visual style continuous. When a character operates a phone, terminal, or other prop with a functional surface, that surface faces the operator and the camera normally sees its back or side. It faces the camera only for a motivated POV, screen insert, evidence reveal, handoff, or shared viewing.
[CONTINUITY] Follow storyboard_context_json.continuity. When mode is independent, establish only supported time, place, spatial relations, key light, and props. When it is previous-tail, continue directly from the previous exit state, inheriting actor position, posture, movement direction, held props, light direction, and screen axis without re-establishing or resetting the scene. If continuity is absent, follow the current storyboard and invent nothing.
[AUDIO POLICY] Follow storyboard_context_json.audio_policy.mode. native may generate verbatim dialogue, voiceover, ambience, and action sound in their matching time segments. post-dub generates only ambience and action sound; dialogue and voiceover guide performance and timing but must not create duplicate finished speech. silent generates no native audio. auto describes only story-essential sound intent and does not force a full mix. On-screen dialogue uses natural lip movement by the visible speaker. Off-screen dialogue must not make another visible character speak. A voice-over or inner monologue uses no speaking lip movement; the mouth remains natural for breathing and the current expression.
5. Anti-grid is the highest composition constraint: the first sentence of the prompt must copy the fixed statement verbatim, and after the exit-frame line required by rule 8 the prompt must end with the same statement repeated verbatim, word for word including punctuation: "The final video must be one single continuous cinematic frame filling the entire screen; no grid, split screen, panels, collage, dividing lines, panel numbers, or any multi-image composition." Immediately after the opening statement, copy verbatim: "The source video must contain no generated subtitles, captions, titles, labels, logos, watermarks, UI text, or any other readable text; all approved text must be added only in post-production." Storyboard paper, white margins, frames, grids, timecodes, text, numbering, arrows, and production notes are guidance only and must not appear in the finished video. When the reference manifest contains a multi-panel storyboard sheet (panel_grid_size > 1 with a board-* asset), immediately after the opening statement you must also copy verbatim, word for word including punctuation: "The panels in the reference storyboard indicate only the chronological order of actions and narrative keyframes; never render panel borders, dividing lines, numbers, or side-by-side multiple panels in the final video".
6. Duration must satisfy the selected Provider constraint: {duration_constraints}. Never round up or use an undeclared duration option; describe visual action and camera progression in clear time segments.
7. Avoid repeated quality buzzwords. Keep only details that support narrative and visual continuity.
8. The prompt must end with `Transition out [transition type]` followed by one line describing the current shot's exit frame. Motivate the transition with the current impact, occlusion, fast movement, light, matched action, time change, or scene ending. Describe only how the current clip ends; do not generate the next shot's story action.
9. Compile the composition contract across the storyboard-sheet timeline. When the structured storyboard or the gridCells' video prompts contain composition_contract (or photographyPlan.composition / visual_plan.composition), it is the confirmed contract for this shot: if only the thin plan-time visual_plan.composition exists (primary/secondary/reason/evidence), do not demand the 11 geometry fields — derive missing geometry from the current storyboard and source text, falling back to legacy handling where it cannot be derived, and never invent field values; the primary/secondary composition codes and pattern names are indexes only — never write them into the final prompt, and never fall back on bare abstractions such as "cinematic", "oppressive", or "lonely". Deliver the contract geometry along cellIndex — the establishment beat (first frame) states the read_order first-glance landing, subject_placement, eyeline_room, depth_layers, and light_and_color_geometry key-light direction; action beats follow action_vector for approach/recede, entry/exit edges, and occlusion yielding, arranged as full anticipation—contact—result—reaction causality with the contact point and force result each in a clear time segment; the resolution beat lands on tail_state's briefly stable pose (position, facing, residual tension), a static contract states locked-off camera explicitly, and unrequested push-in, orbit, or drift is forbidden; when the sheet contains cuts, keep screen direction, entry/exit edges, and axis consistent on both sides, and any axis crossing needs composition-contract or cinemaKnowledge support plus a readable transition; previous-tail continuity inherits the previous exit state directly. Vertical 9:16: stage relationships through front/back depth, over-shoulder, or vertical placement rather than squeezing side by side; keep key faces, evidence props, and contact points in the upper-middle safe area with both eyes readable; split crowd scenes across beats/time segments into crowd establishment, protagonist action, and reaction/result rather than lining up many figures horizontally. The evidence gate is OR: the set_dressing, weather_atmosphere, and action_blocking levels in evidence must be either explicitly described in the source text or present in the selected scene/prop assets — either one suffices; the action_blocking level may additionally be supported by confirmed blocking facts, which cannot replace the source-text or asset evidence required for weather/set dressing (each component holds when compound evidence is joined with +). When any is unsupported, stop compiling and produce no prompt (empty prompt string), name the missing evidence in errors, and return the shot as unresolved to revise-drama-storyboards for evidence or a pattern change. Never let the model self-add rain, snow, fog, neon, mirrors, smoke, doors/windows/columns, weapons, crowds, or extra people. Give each ai_risks risk a visible safeguard (occlusion under one third with eyes complete, clean negative space, clear contact point, 10% margin at motion edges, cross-shot direction consistency). This rule's content goes in the body between the fixed opening and closing statements required by rule 5; never move or rewrite those fixed statements; storyboard paper, frames, arrows, and numbering remain planning-only references; add no slots and do not change asset referencing.
